## 5. Search planning module: lost person behaviour, scenario analysis, POI reference

**Goal.** Give the search manager a first search plan within minutes of a callout, grounded
in the *UK Missing Person Behaviour Study* (UKMPBS, Centre for Search Research, Perkins,
Roberts & Feeney 2011; plus the later UK update and ISRID where UK data is thin), rather
than drawing targets from memory.

**Inputs.**

- Subject profile from the console: category (despondent, dementia, child by age band,
  walker/hiker, mental health, autism, elderly, water-related, etc.), age, mobility, mental
  state, intention if known.
- Initial Planning Point (IPP): last known position or place last seen, dropped on the map.
- Time missing, weather, daylight (already in state), tide (already in state) for
  coastal/foreshore cases.

**Behaviour reference and plan card — done.** `shared/lpb/` holds the live MISPER-UK
dataset (regenerated by `scripts/fetch-misper.mjs`), the 2011 study's terrain/sex tables
and scenarios, the ISRID autism figures and the reflex tasks. The Subject form records
category, terrain and the despondent factors; the *Behaviour* tab is a plan card (rings
25/50/75/90 %, where found, condition with modifiers, scenarios, tasks, warnings) with a
source tag on every number. Still open from this item: recording our own outcomes at close
(the Irish evidence base), scenarios with POA/POD, POIs from OSM. misper.uk and the study
state no reuse licence, so confirm with The Centre for Search Research before wider
distribution.

**Scenario analysis.** The search manager records 2 to 5 scenarios (e.g. "entered water at
the pier", "walked the cliff path east", "returned to car park / left area") and weights them
by consensus (simple 0–100 split, Mattson-style). Each scenario maps to the behaviour profile
and to POIs, producing segments with an initial probability of area (POA). After a team
reports a segment searched with an estimated probability of detection (POD), POA is updated
(Bayesian, `POA' = POA × (1 − POD)` normalised) and the ranking refreshes. Show the ranked
segment list with cumulative POS.

**POI-based reference.** Pull points of interest around the IPP from OpenStreetMap
(Overpass, cached per station: piers, slipways, beaches, cliffs, car parks, bus stops, pubs,
shelters, lifebuoy stations, benches, paths, drainage, buildings) and from a station-curated
list in `config.yaml` (known hazard spots, regular routes). Rank POIs per scenario using the
find-location statistics and distance quartiles; one click turns a POI into a target on the
existing targets layer.

**Map output.** Distance rings around the IPP (done, console only), scenario-coloured segment
polygons, ranked POI markers. All reuse `web/src/search/layers.ts` and the existing
`targets` API, so segments become assignable targets with no new tracking logic.

**Data model.** `incident_subject` (profile), `scenarios` (incident, label, weight),
`segments` (incident, scenario, geometry, poa, pod, searched_at, team_id → existing targets
row), `poi_cache` (station, osm_id, kind, geometry, fetched_at). Audit every weight change.

**Out of scope / caveats.** Not a replacement for IRCG search procedure or the SMC's
judgement; the UK figures are land SAR statistics, so coastal drift is handled by the
existing tide/wind panels, not by this module. No drift modelling (that would be a separate
SAROPS-style item).

**Acceptance.** From a test callout at Howth pier, enter "walker, 65, mobile", two
scenarios, and get within two minutes: rings, ranked POIs, three segments assigned to Alpha
teams, and a POA table that updates after a segment is marked searched at 70 % POD.

## 6. Deploy over Tailscale, close SSH to the internet

**Why.** CI deploys by SSH from GitHub runners, whose addresses change, so port 22 on the
VPS firewall (`howth-cg-ops` group) is currently open to any IPv4 address. Login is
key-only and the deploy key is forced to `/opt/howth-cg/deploy.sh`, but SSH should not face
the internet.

**Plan.**
1. Tailscale on the VPS (`tailscale up --ssh=false`), tagged `tag:howth-cg`; add the install
   to `deploy/cloud-init.yaml` for rebuilds.
2. ACL: only `tag:ci` may reach `tag:howth-cg` on port 22; admins via their own devices.
3. OAuth client in the Tailscale admin with the `tag:ci` tag; store `TS_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID` and
   `TS_OAUTH_SECRET` as repo secrets.
4. `ci.yml` deploy job: add `tailscale/github-action` before the SSH step, and set
   `DEPLOY_HOST` to the VPS's tailnet name or 100.x address.
5. Vultr firewall: delete the "ssh any (GitHub deploy, key-only)" rule; keep or drop the
   admin-IP rule.

**Acceptance.** A merge to main deploys through the tailnet, and `nc -zv 192.248.159.129 22`
from outside the tailnet times out.

## 7. Incident contacts: other agencies and next of kin

**Why.** During a search the manager rings and is rung by the Gardaí, MRCC Dublin, Dublin Fire Brigade, the Coast Guard helicopter, neighbouring units and the family. Today those numbers and names live on paper or in someone's phone, and the log of who was told what is lost at stand-down.

**Plan.**
1. Per-incident contacts: role (Garda, MRCC, ambulance, other unit, next of kin, reporting person, other), name, organisation, phone, notes, and a reference such as the Garda PULSE or incident number.
2. A station directory of standing contacts (Howth Garda station, MRCC Dublin, Dublin Fire Brigade, Rescue 116, RNLI Howth), so most entries are one click from a list rather than typed.
3. A short contact log per incident: time, who, which way, one line of what was said. It appears in the history timeline and the end-of-mission report.
4. **Privacy:** next of kin and reporting person are private like home and significant places. The console and the report show them; display keys and crew phones never receive them. Agency contacts may show to crew leaders. Audit every view and change.
5. Retention follows the incident record. Next-of-kin details are cleared once the incident is closed and the report filed, per GDPR data minimisation.

**Acceptance.** From a test tasking, add the Garda contact from the directory and a next of kin by hand, log two calls, and see both in the report. The TV and a crew phone show neither the next of kin nor the call log.

## 8. Terrain shipped ready-built for the station's area, after a review of sources

**Why.** Search areas read terrain from local files, so nothing downloads during a callout. But those files are built on the server, from Copernicus, GSI/OPW and TII LiDAR downloads, Overpass and a 375 MB OpenStreetMap extract. The first build on the VPS hit Overpass outages. A new station, a rebuilt server or a lost data volume all depend on outside servers being up at that moment. Terrain should arrive with the deploy, built and checked beforehand, never fetched on the live server.

**Step 1: review the terrain sources, paid ones included, before building the package.** For each source, record resolution, coverage of the station area (Dublin city centre to Skerries), licence, whether the licence allows shipping the data in a build, cost, how often it is updated, and how well it shows cliffs and towns. Candidates, each to be checked:

| Need | Free | Paid or by agreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground height | GSI/OPW and TII LiDAR, 1–2 m, partial coverage (used now); Copernicus GLO-30, a 30 m surface model (used now); EU-DEM 25 m; FABDEM 30 m, with buildings and trees removed (better in towns; free for non-commercial use) | Tailte Éireann (formerly OSi) 5 m DTM and height data; commercial 5 m DTMs for Ireland, such as Intermap NEXTMap; Bluesky or Airbus WorldDEM |
| Land cover | OpenStreetMap (used now); National Land Cover Map 2018 (Tailte Éireann and EPA); CORINE 2018, 100 m | Tailte Éireann Prime2 polygons |
| Coast and cliffs | OpenStreetMap (used now) | Tailte Éireann high-water mark and Prime2 cliffs |
| Place names | OpenStreetMap (used now) | Tailte Éireann gazetteer; Eircode lookup (ECAD), which also unlocks Eircodes in the position box |

Check first whether the Coast Guard, as a public body under the Department of Transport, already has Tailte Éireann data through a public-sector mapping agreement. That could make the 5 m DTM and Prime2 available at no new cost. Output: a short recommendation with costs, and a source for each layer.

**Step 2: build a terrain package per station area, ahead of time.**
1. The terrain build becomes an offline step, run on a workstation or in a manual CI job, never on the live server. Its input is the station's area and the chosen sources. Its output is a versioned package: the grid, `places.json`, a manifest of sources and licences, and checksums. The current area is about 60 MB.
2. The package is published as a GitHub release asset, or baked into the image if every licence allows it. `deploy.sh` installs the package that matches the release and checks its checksums. It never builds.
3. The server only loads the package. Settings → Terrain shows the package version, area, sources and date, and no longer has a Build button on the live server.
4. Validation runs before a package is published: the spot heights at Howth summit (171 m), the pier and the Baily, the sea mask, place search, and a test search area. A package that fails is not published.
5. Rebuilds are planned, for example every quarter for OpenStreetMap changes, or when a better source is licensed. They are never done during a callout.

**Acceptance.** A fresh server deployed with no internet access beyond the release has terrain and place search working at first start. The Settings page names the package and its sources. No terrain download code runs on the live server.
